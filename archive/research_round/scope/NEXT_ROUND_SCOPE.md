## The scientific question that stays central

How does the distribution of neuronal operating points generated by balance
shape the covariance geometry and participation ratio of a two-population EI
network? The theory should retain conditional local dynamics, distinguish
E–E, E–I, and I–I covariance energies, and explain what is lost by averaging
local neuronal laws too early. Participation ratio measures activity
dimensionality; a claim about computational performance would require a
separate task-based experiment.

Nischal's handoff said that the core idea and results were in decent shape,
but needed work to flesh them out and extract a paper. He asked Aniket to
understand the core results, round off the analytics gaps, and obtain good
simulation-versus-theory figures. The original project-direction note also
identifies the 60:40 discrepancy and the nonuniform degree test as the next
useful decisions. It allows a supported domain of validity to resolve the
discrepancy when a predictive correction is unavailable.

| Conditional correction | Develop further finite-size or connected-response terms when a persistent residual identifies what the calculation would test. | A parameter-only prediction or a test that distinguishes plausible failure mechanisms. |
| Deferred extension | Complete all second-order vertices, the full quenched fluctuation distribution, rare-attractor probabilities, full covariance spectra, or a broad critical simulation campaign. | A separate objective if the core paper later requires or motivates it. |

The scalar disorder–curvature crossover remains a potentially useful result.
Its current role is an analytical mechanism and a possible supporting figure.
It becomes a main-paper claim only if its connection to nonuniform EI
networks and its evidence justify that role. It does not replace the original
local-law-to-geometry question. The three-halves exponent alone does not
isolate heterogeneity: a homogeneous biased operating point also supplies
curvature.

## The next concrete work, in order

1. Specify a compact prospective validation protocol shared by the composition
   and nonuniform-degree comparisons. Use fresh connectivity realizations,
   record every outcome, and retain both the mean PR and the ratio of ensemble
   mean moments. Check recording duration and block dependence at selected
   points. Choose further sampling from the observed uncertainty at the
   decisive comparisons; roughly 100 networks per point is not a default
   requirement.
2. Complete the nonuniform degree comparison and the focused fraction/size
   diagnostic. Compare predicted local statistics with measurements before
   judging the pair calculation. Substituting measured local laws into the
   pair calculation is a diagnostic, kept distinct from the parameter-only
   prediction.
3. Use those results to choose the analytic work that remains necessary. If
   local laws converge but pair energy does not, investigate the collective
   closure. If local gains and backgrounds remain wrong, investigate the
   one-site law. If disagreement tracks the recording procedure, repair the
   observable estimate before assigning a dynamical explanation.
4. Assemble the core paper figures and claim/evidence table. Add a crossover
   panel only when the bounded feasibility check and the EI connection support
   it. An inaccessible near-critical regime is reported as an analytical
   prediction and limitation; it does not halt the rest of the paper.

The degree/composition controls must state exactly which matrices and
parameters remain fixed. Static heterogeneity, fluctuation amplitude,
recurrent gain moments, and covariance energy should remain visible alongside
PR, since numerator–denominator cancellations can conceal ingredient errors.
Classify fixed points, periodic activity, and fluctuating states consistently;
selected branch diagnostics do not establish basin probabilities.

## Stopping criterion

A sufficient next paper version contains one transparent conditional EI
construction, the nonuniform degree test, and a supported account of the
fraction discrepancy: either a predictive correction or a quantitatively
delimited regime of validity. A small set of simulation-versus-theory figures
must discriminate the retained heterogeneity mechanism from premature
averaging. Unresolved corrections remain explicit limitations.

Completing every finite-size coefficient, every rare dynamical outcome, or a
new universal crossover theory is not a prerequisite. If a limitation prevents
a proposed claim, narrow that claim or do the specific calculation it needs.
Keep all derivations, unsuccessful attempts, numerical checks, and decisions
in this archive.
