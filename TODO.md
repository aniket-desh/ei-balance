# Research to-do list for the January draft

The dense sweep, particle solver, fixed-size readout pilot, and first spectral experiment are in place. The January draft can proceed on the central result that heterogeneous operating points shape covariance and activity dimension. The open question for the next round is whether a change in composition also gives an excitatory readout more independent dimensions.

## 1. Immediate theory

- [x] **1a. Explain the remaining error along the degree sweep.** At 2,000 neurons, joint dimension per neuron falls from 0.154 to 0.0187 across the 12 measured dispersions. The Gaussian theory predicts the curvature, but overshoots at the low endpoint and undershoots at the high one. At each dispersion, use $D/N=\nu^2/\mathcal E$ to separate the contribution of mean temporal rate variance from covariance energy. Break the latter into E–E, twice E–I, and I–I. Compare the discrepancies across individual networks to identify what a better theory must predict. [Degree results](experiments/dense_sweeps/experiments/dense-ei/README.md) · [Completed residual analysis](experiments/degree_residual/README.md)
- [x] **1b. Derive the optimality prediction for a fixed readout.** The conditional theory peaks near 88.7% E for $D_E/N$, the total number of E dimensions divided by network size. For a downstream population sampling 100 E cells, the relevant quantity is the PR of those 100 cells. Start from the expected diagonal and distinct-pair covariance contributions of a uniform subset. Estimate the change in readout PR from 80% to 88% E and quantify the error from taking a ratio of expected moments. Repeat for I. This supplies the effect size for 2a. [Population PR calculation](figures/population_pr.py) · [Readout pilot](experiments/readout_pilot/README.md) · [Completed fixed-readout derivation](experiments/readout_theory/README.md)
- [x] **1c. Extend the particle theory if 1a points to local gains or variances.** The current solver varies each cell’s mean input and shares one temporal input variance within each population. Substituting a cell’s measured temporal variance improved gain prediction in all 18 network-by-population comparisons. Predict mean input and temporal variance jointly from connectivity, then test gains and E-only, I-only, and joint PR on held-out networks against the Gaussian baseline. [Particle comparison](experiments/particle_pilot/README.md) · [Held-out gains and dimension](experiments/degree_residual/holdout_scores.json)

The reviewed design uses 624 fresh seed triplets. [Frozen protocol](experiments/readout_confirmation/EXPERIMENT.md)
