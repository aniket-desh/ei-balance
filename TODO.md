The theory, independent experiments, and figures in sections 1–3a are ready for Nischal’s review. The composition experiment resolves a geometric trade-off for a fixed 100-cell readout. ## 1. Immediate theory

- [x] **1a. Explain the remaining error along the degree sweep.** Partitioned each network’s log dimension error into temporal rate variance and the E–E, E–I, and I–I covariance energies. Excess squared covariance between distinct E cells explains most of the shortfall at low degree dispersion. At high dispersion the balance of terms changes across cohorts. [Derivation and measurements](experiments/degree_residual/README.md)
- [x] **1b. Derive the prediction for a fixed readout.** Calculated the expected squared trace and covariance energy for 100 cells sampled without replacement. Their ratio predicts a gain of 0.462 E dimensions and a loss of 1.166 I dimensions from 80% to 88% E. The predicted fraction maximizing total E dimension per network neuron is 0.8874. [Derivation, approximation error, and pilot](experiments/readout_theory/README.md)
- [x] **1c. Test whether cell-specific temporal variances improve the particle theory.** Froze the predictions before measuring nine fresh networks. At degree dispersions 1 and 2, the extension improved local gain estimates in all 12 population-by-network comparisons, yet increased error in the population dimensions. Retain the Gaussian prediction for the degree figure. [Closure and held-out scores](experiments/degree_residual/README.md#held-out-results)

## 2. Immediate experiments

- [x] **2a. Size an independent composition test.** Used the four-seed pilot to choose 624 fresh matched triplets. The primary endpoint was the change in 100-cell E-readout PR. Five secondary endpoints covered the I readout, two pair energies, and both flanks of total E dimension. [Sizing calculation](experiments/readout_theory/README.md#size-of-the-independent-test) · [Frozen protocol](experiments/readout_confirmation/EXPERIMENT.md)
- [x] **2b. Test the readout trade-off and the predicted peak.** Ran all 1,872 networks. From 80% to 88% E, the E readout gained 0.592 dimensions and the I readout lost 0.734. E–E pair energy fell, I–I pair energy rose, and total E dimension per network neuron was higher at 88% than at either 80% or 94%. The I decrease was smaller than predicted. [Estimates, intervals, and reproducible records](experiments/readout_confirmation/README.md)
- [x] **2c. Replicate the fixed-point spectral analysis.** Applied a prespecified root-selection rule to 24 fresh networks. In the 20 that fluctuated, the leading fixed-point subspace captured a median 39.5% of squared rate-covariance energy against 8.4% in the shuffled control. Projecting out this subspace increased PR in 18 of 20 networks. [Spectral results and root sensitivity](experiments/spectral_replication/README.md)
