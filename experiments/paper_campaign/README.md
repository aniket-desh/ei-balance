# Larger sweeps for the paper

This campaign reruns every simulation comparison the paper depends on with more settings, more networks and a wider range of sizes: 1,694 fresh networks from 500 to 16,000 neurons, plus 141 networks of 250 to 2,000 neurons for the fixed-point mode analysis. The conditional theory tracks the degree sweep from fixed in-degree to three times the Bernoulli dispersion, the whole composition path from 50% to 94% excitatory cells, single-neuron variance and gain laws, and two-lag pair moments. In the degree sweep its largest error is a 10 to 12% deficit in dimension at dispersions up to 0.5 and 2,000 neurons; at fixed in-degree that deficit shrinks steadily with network size. At 16,000 neurons it is 2.2%. Away from the reference parameters the error reaches +34%, at target rate 0.15.

## Results

**The degree curve.** Mean dimension per neuron falls from 0.1567 at fixed in-degree to 0.0162 at dispersion 2 and 0.0079 at dispersion 3, a 9.65-fold change from 0 to 2 against 10.34 predicted. Below dispersion 0.5 the measured mean sits 10 to 12% under the prediction; from dispersion 1 upward it is within one standard error or two (−3.9 ± 2.4% at 1, −3.4 ± 4.0% at 2). The root-mean-square error of the cell-mean log dimension over all 15 dispersions is 0.108 for the conditional theory, 0.85 when spectra are averaged first and 1.64 when gains are averaged first. The earlier +30% excess at dispersion 2 came from three networks; with 24 the excess is gone, and the spread across networks there is about 20%. [Dimension](figures/degree_dimension.pdf) · [Ingredients](figures/degree_ingredients.pdf) · [Ratios](figures/degree_ratios.pdf) · [Residual partition](figures/degree_residual_partition.pdf)

**The fixed in-degree deficit is a finite-size effect.** At dispersion 0 the median relative error of D/N is −34.6%, −16.4%, −9.8%, −5.9%, −3.7% and −2.2% at 500, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000, 8,000 and 16,000 neurons. Over this range the dimension error falls roughly as N^−0.8, the covariance-energy and E–E pair-energy errors as N^−0.7, and the rate-variance and E-gain errors more slowly (N^−0.5 and N^−0.4); these exponents are descriptive fits. The across-neuron variance of mean inputs, which the leading theory sets to zero at fixed in-degree, falls as N^−1.1. Part of that variance comes from the stochastic rounding of non-integer mean counts, whose dispersion also shrinks with size (at most 0.021 in any block at 500 neurons and 0.0004 at 16,000), so the exponent mixes the two effects. At 16,000 neurons the errors at dispersions 1 and 2 are −0.2 ± 2.3% and +3.7 ± 3.7%. At dispersions 1 and 2 and at 60:40 the median dimension error is within two bootstrap standard errors of zero from 2,000 neurons upward (within one at dispersion 1), so no trend is resolved there; at dispersion 0.5 the error is not monotone in size. Networks of 500 neurons are a different regime: 11 of 240 sit at a fixed point for the whole recording, 3 more settle to one during it, and several more fluctuate weakly. [Scaling](figures/size_scaling.pdf) · [Exponents](figures/size_fixed_degree_exponents.pdf) · [Ingredients](figures/size_ingredients.pdf)

**Single-neuron laws.** Plotting each neuron's temporal rate variance and time-averaged gain against its time-averaged input puts the measurements on the conditional curves v_a(z) and α_a(z), and the distribution of mean inputs is close to the predicted Gaussian at dispersions 1 and 2 (its variance is 3 to 7% above prediction). The E squared gain is the one ingredient with a persistent offset: 8 to 10% below prediction at every dispersion at 2,000 neurons (the I squared gain is 2 to 6% below), shrinking slowly with size. [Local laws](figures/local_laws.pdf) · [In-degree and inputs](figures/local_degree_and_inputs.pdf) · [Kernels](figures/temporal_kernels.pdf)

**Composition.** With 48 matched seeds at each of 13 fractions, the measured-to-predicted ratio of D/N stays between 0.91 and 1.04 and the E–I temporal alignment follows its prediction from 0.905 at 50% E to 0.678 at 94% (predicted 0.899 to 0.688). Total E dimension per network neuron is largest at 86% and 88% E among the sampled fractions (0.0348 and 0.0342), against a predicted maximum at 88.7%; the seed-paired rise from 80% to 88% is +0.0021 (pointwise 95% interval +0.0003 to +0.0038; predicted +0.0015; the 624-triplet experiment measured +0.0018). The I–I pair energy rises toward high E fraction as predicted: +0.332 from 80% to 94% (interval +0.242 to +0.422; predicted +0.327). The 100-cell I readout loses 4.46 dimensions over the same step (interval −5.52 to −3.41; predicted −4.69). The smaller contrasts between 80% and 88% are not resolved by 48 pairs and rest on the 624-triplet experiment. [Moments](figures/composition_moments.pdf) · [Dimensions](figures/composition_dimensions.pdf) · [Paired changes](figures/composition_paired_changes.pdf) · [Readouts by size](figures/composition_readout_curves.pdf)

**Two-lag pair moments.** The manuscript's lag figure uses five historical networks and an estimator that multiplies separately centered blocks; it shows unexplained negative lobes. Here each map multiplies covariance estimates from two disjoint halves of the recording, so the estimation noise of one factor does not multiply itself. The mean maps correlate with the parameter-only prediction at 0.99 or better for E–E, E–I and I–I pairs at dispersions 0, 1 and 2, and the most negative value in any 8-network mean map is under 1% of its peak (single-network maps reach −2%, −8% and −24% of the peak at dispersions 0, 1 and 2). The relative Frobenius error is 7 to 16% at dispersions 1 and 2 and 21 to 25% at dispersion 0, where the zero-lag value is 18 to 22% above prediction, the lag-resolved form of the pair-energy excess at fixed in-degree. The E–E zero-lag value is also 19% above prediction at dispersion 1, and all three are 5 to 7% below at dispersion 2. [Dispersion 1](figures/lag_maps_chi1.pdf) · [Dispersion 0](figures/lag_maps_chi0.pdf) · [Dispersion 2](figures/lag_maps_chi2.pdf) · [Same-neuron sources](figures/lag_sources.pdf)

**Recording protocol and integration step.** The separately centered estimate exceeds the mean-difference estimate by 4.0%, 2.0% and 1.0% at window lengths 500, 1,000 and 2,000 (dispersion 0), independent of recording length, as the centering identity predicts. Averaged over eight networks, the mean-difference dimension from 8,000 leak times is within 0.4% of the 32,000 value at all four settings; single networks differ by up to 0.7% at dispersion 0 and by 2 to 5% elsewhere. Requiring an empty window between estimator windows changes the mean D/N of the long recordings by less than 0.15%. Halving the step changes the mean D/N by −0.5 ± 0.3% at dispersion 0 and −1.0 ± 0.4% at dispersion 1, and by less than one standard error (about 1.3%) at dispersion 2 and at 60:40. [Duration and window](figures/record_duration_and_block.pdf) · [Convergence](figures/record_convergence.pdf) · [Gap and step](figures/record_gap_and_step.pdf)

**Other couplings and rates.** Scaling the coupling variance by 0.75, 1.5, 2 and 3 leaves the median dimension 6%, 9%, 11% and 13% below prediction at 2,000 neurons, with the rate variance within 3% and the energy 8 to 17% high, the same pattern as the fixed in-degree deficit. At target rate 0.5 the dimension is within 2%. The theory does poorly at two settings at this size. At rate 0.15 the rate variance is 46% above prediction and the dimension 34% above. At coupling scale 0.5, the one setting close to the transition (predicted response radius 0.96), two of 16 networks barely fluctuate and one settles to a fixed point, and D/N scatters from −63% to +236% around a prediction of 0.0027. [Coupling and rate](figures/generality_coupling_and_rate.pdf)

**Chaos.** Of 280 measured largest Lyapunov exponents, 276 are positive in every one of their eight time blocks (0.016 to 0.38). Three are zero within their block-to-block scatter: two 500-neuron networks at dispersion 2, one of which fluctuates at 93% of the predicted amplitude, and one network at coupling scale 0.5. The one measured on a fixed-point network is −0.084. Cell means run from 0.065 at dispersion 3 to 0.23 at fixed in-degree and 0.37 at coupling scale 3. [Exponents](figures/check_lyapunov_all.pdf)

**Fixed-point modes.** The three comparisons of the 24-network replication hold at every size and dispersion tried. In all 135 fluctuating networks analysed, the rank correlation between a mode's real eigenvalue and its recorded input variance exceeds that network's shuffle median, and the leading tenth of fixed-point directions holds more squared rate-covariance energy than the gain-matched shuffle. For Bernoulli networks the median correlation rises with size (0.22, 0.36, 0.43 and 0.50 at 250, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 neurons) while the median energy fraction falls (0.64, 0.40, 0.35, 0.35, against 0.14, 0.08, 0.07, 0.06 for the shuffle). Projecting out that subspace raises PR in 17 of 26, 29 of 32, 30 of 32 and 13 of 13 networks, with median ratios 1.07, 1.19, 1.23 and 1.31. At 1,000 neurons the correlation is much stronger at fixed in-degree (median 0.79) than at dispersion 2 (0.30), while the energy fraction is smaller (0.13 against 0.48; shuffles 0.03 and 0.10). Six of the 32 networks at 250 neurons did not fluctuate by the replication's criterion and are excluded. Three of the 16 networks at 2,000 neurons exceeded the one-hour limit in the fixed-point search and were not analysed; because inclusion depends on the search finishing, the 2,000-neuron medians describe the other 13. Statements in `paper/results.tex` and `paper/main.tex` that these data revise. None of them has been edited.

* "The N = 2000 endpoint means are 10.2% below and 30.3% above their respective predictions." With 24 networks per endpoint the errors are −9.9 ± 0.3% and −3.4 ± 4.0%.
* "Increasing size does not uniformly improve every comparison." At fixed in-degree the dimension, energy, pair-energy, rate-variance and gain errors all shrink monotonically from 500 neurons upward. At dispersions 1 and 2 the dimension error is within about one standard error of zero from 2,000 neurons, so no trend is resolved there; the E gain error stays 6 to 10% low at every size, the dispersion-1 energy and pair-energy errors are still +8 to +10% at 8,000 neurons, and at dispersion 0.5 the dimension error is not monotone in size. The sentence stands for those settings.
* "Both intervals include zero, so these measurements leave the proposed E–E decrease and I–I increase unresolved." The I–I increase is resolved between 80% and each of 90%, 92% and 94% E, and the E–E pair energy is resolved as higher at 50% to 65% E than at 80% (pointwise 95% intervals, which also exclude zero under a 12-fraction Bonferroni correction). The 80% to 88% contrasts themselves still rest on the 624-triplet experiment.
* "Negative unequal-lag lobes remain unexplained" (historical lag appendix). The 8-network mean cross-fitted maps have no negative values beyond 1% of the peak.
* "Likewise, selected examples of weak chaos, fixed points, and periodic activity establish a classification issue, not their ensemble probabilities." This campaign gives counts: 276 of 280 measured Lyapunov exponents are positive in every time block; 14 of 240 networks at 500 neurons end at a fixed point, as does 1 of 16 at 2,000 neurons with coupling scale 0.5 and none of the other 1,426 networks up to 8,000 neurons.
* The results section's statement that the mean squared time-averaged gains at N = 2,000 lie "about 6–8% below their predictions" holds for E cells across the whole degree sweep (8 to 10% here; I cells 2 to 6%) and decays slowly with size.

## Measurements

Each network is simulated and measured by the archived engine ([runtime/engine_v4.py](../runtime/engine_v4.py), native constant-weight action), with the published protocol: midpoint step 0.05, 500 leak times of burn-in, 8,000 recorded and sampled every 0.25, and the four-window mean-difference estimator with window length 500. The retained trajectory then supplies the extra measurements in [extras.py](extras.py): K-cell readouts with the confirmation experiment's priority rule, dense rate and input autocovariances of up to 192 sampled neurons per population, cross-fitted two-lag maps on those neurons, eigenvalues of the whole-record covariance, and the largest Lyapunov exponent of the midpoint map on a subset of seeds. [Manifest](jobs.json) · [N = 16,000 extension](jobs_extension.json) · [Runner](run.py) · [Theory](theory.py)

A network that ends its recording at a stable fixed point (final drift below 1e−10) has no stationary PR. Those networks are flagged in `networks.csv` (`fluctuating = 0`, with `state` telling whether they sat there for the whole recording or settled during it) and counted in `summary.json`: 11 at a fixed point throughout, all at 500 neurons, and 4 that settled during the recording, three at 500 neurons and one at 2,000 neurons with coupling scale 0.5. They are excluded from every average. Means carry one across-network standard error. The size series uses across-network medians with bootstrap standard errors because a few weakly fluctuating small networks have ill-conditioned ratios.

## Checks

These four are rerun by [validate.py](validate.py) and recorded in [validation.json](validation.json).

* Replaying an archived dense-sweep network through the new runner on the machine that produced the archive reproduced its record exactly (zero difference in D/N and block energies at all four estimator windows), so the wrapper does not change the engine.
* A readout made of an entire population returned the engine's block trace and energy to 7e−15.
* The lag-map code agreed with a brute-force loop over pairs and lags to 5e−11, and the two-lag prediction at zero lag equals the predicted pair energies to 5e−10.
* The theory helper reproduces the archived degree predictions to 1e−15, the readout ratios of the readout-theory table, and the manuscript's averaging-control values.
* Every record and array on every machine was compared by SHA-256 with the local copy before the machine was stopped: no mismatches in 3,364 files of the main campaign, 24 of the N = 16,000 extension and 283 of the fixed-point cohort. No network failed.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, with the project installed and a C++ compiler:

```sh
python experiments/paper_campaign/prepare.py              # writes jobs.json (1,682 networks)
python experiments/paper_campaign/prepare.py --extension  # writes jobs_extension.json (12 networks at N = 16,000)
python experiments/paper_campaign/run.py --part 1/7 --workers 30 --memory-gib 56 --scratch-gib 100
python experiments/paper_campaign/run.py --manifest jobs_extension.json --part 1/4 --workers 3 --memory-gib 58 --scratch-gib 112
python experiments/paper_campaign/spectral.py --workers 30
python experiments/paper_campaign/theory_curves.py
python experiments/paper_campaign/summarize.py && python experiments/paper_campaign/report.py
for s in degree size composition record_generality local_temporal extra checks spectral gallery; do python experiments/paper_campaign/plots/$s.py; done
```

The repository tracks the code, the figures with their captions, and the tables behind them: `networks.csv` (one row per network), `windows.csv` (every estimator window), `theory.csv`, `theory_curves.json`, `summary.json` and `spectral/summary.json`. The per-network records and arrays (`records/`, `arrays/`, and the raw `spectral/` measurements, about 125 MB) stay local. The degree, size, composition, recording, coupling and rate figures redraw from the tracked tables; the local-law, kernel, lag-map, spectrum, overview and fixed-point figures, and the in-degree and operating-point checks, also need the local arrays, which the commands above regenerate.

`run.py --part i/7` selects a deterministic share of the manifest, so seven machines never share a network. The figures read `networks.csv`, `windows.csv`, `arrays/`, `theory_curves.json` and `spectral/`; the local-law, lag-map and overview figures also re-solve the theory through `theory.py` (about two seconds per setting, cached in `theory_cache/`).

## Compute

Eight 32-vCPU `cpu5c` machines in EUR-IS-1 on the CBAI Runpod account, at $1.12 per hour each, ran for 9.24 machine-hours in total: about $10.35 of compute, estimated from the uptime each machine reported when it was stopped. The 1,682 networks of the main campaign took between 15 and 34 minutes per machine; the twelve N = 16,000 networks took 51 to 55 minutes on four machines, and the fixed-point cohort an hour on one. One machine was replaced after its host proved about five times slower than the others; its unfinished audit file is kept and none of its output was used. All eight machines are stopped and hold no data (a CPU machine has only a container disk, which is cleared on stop); they can be deleted. [Machines, uptimes and checksums](cloud_execution.json)

Each machine was meant to carry a 2.5-hour self-stop timer from launch. A bug in the launch script armed it on one machine only; the other six ran for about 40 minutes without a timer before this was noticed and corrected. No machine reached its timer.
