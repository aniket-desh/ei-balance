(a) Measured joint participation ratio divided by network size against in-degree dispersion at 80% E. The solid curve is the Gaussian conditional theory. Shape marks network size, faint points show network draws, and half-filled and filled markers give the original and dense cohort means, respectively. (b) The mean log ratio of measured to predicted dimension per neuron in the 2,000-cell dense sweep equals the sum of the mean rate-variance and covariance-energy terms. (c) The energy term separates into distinct E–E pairs, the E diagonal, both E–I blocks, and the I–I block including its diagonal. Terms use the integral of the inverse total energy along a straight line between predicted and measured block energies in each network. Excess distinct E–E covariance gives the largest contribution to the low-dispersion deficit. (d) At dispersion 2, the original 2,000-cell cohort's excess dimension comes mainly from rate variance, and the three held-out draws also have a positive rate-variance contribution. The dense cohort instead has less distinct-pair covariance than predicted. Each marker in (b–d) averages three network draws. Bars throughout show one between-network standard error. The horizontal axis in (a–c) is spaced by the square root of dispersion. Panels (a–c) use the original or dense records described above (24 original and 45 dense networks in all). Panel (d) adds three independent held-out networks. All estimates use 8,000 time units, 500-unit windows, and zero minimum gap.
