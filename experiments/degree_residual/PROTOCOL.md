# Held-out test of a graph-conditioned variance closure

Freeze the following choices before opening recordings from seeds 15101–15103. Use the original 2,000-cell degree ensemble, χ = 0, 1, 2, and its specified drives. At each χ, reconstruct the exact connectivity from its seed. Both graph models fix each population's average rate at 0.3 and temporal input variance at the parameter-only Gaussian prediction. The two-site cavity retains its original population response and the normalized temporal kernel shape in each class.

1. Solve the mean of each cell from its centered recurrent input and the target-rate constraint. In the shared-variance control each cell receives its population variance.
2. In the joint model update each cell's variance from the sum of squared, ensemble-centered incoming weights times the presynaptic filtered rate covariance. Normalize these variances to the same population average. Iterate the mean and variance together. Evaluate positive, converged solutions and retain all failures.
3. Primary local endpoint: within each network and class, predict the temporal mean gain of individual cells and their temporal input variances. Measure mean squared error against the full-record cell statistics and report squared-error differences between joint and shared models. Check population mean squared gains with distinct recording blocks. For E-only, I-only, and joint PR compare the mean absolute log error for the joint, graph-shared, and Gaussian theories. Use independent networks as the uncertainty unit, with paired seeds across χ. Cells and multiple χ settings from the same seed do not count as extra independent replicates.

For the descriptive residual analysis use the 24 original and 45 dense degree networks. Decompose each log dimension error along the straight path between predicted and observed covariance block energies, then average the per-network terms within size, dispersion and cohort. The denominator weights are identical for all blocks of one network and sum exactly to its log energy ratio. Use the four-window T8000/B500/gap0 estimator, and repeat with T4000/B500/gap0 and T8000/B500/gap1 where available.
