Increasing the excitatory fraction from 0.80 to 0.88 raised the mean PR of a 100-cell E readout by 0.59 dimensions and lowered that of a 100-cell I readout by 0.73. All networks have N = 2,000 and fixed aggregate couplings and target rates. (a) D_E/N counts the dimensions of the entire E population per network neuron. The hollow diamond marks its parameter-only theoretical maximum at excitatory fraction 0.887. (b) P_aa is N times the mean squared covariance over distinct ordered E–E or I–I pairs. (c) The E and I readouts each sample 100 cells from their own population. Lines in (c) give the ratio of the expected squared trace to the expected covariance energy, including the finite-population variance of the subset trace. The simulated value averages the actual PR of eight uniform readouts within each network. (d) Paired change in each readout PR when the same network seed is compared at 80% and 88% E. Hollow diamonds mark the theoretical changes. Bars in (d) give the two-sided paired 95% interval for the primary E endpoint and the 99% interval for I, one of five secondary contrasts with Bonferroni simultaneous coverage. In (a–c), bars give one standard error across independent network seeds. Open circles in (a,b) show the earlier three-seed means at nine fractions. Filled squares show 624 independent matched seed triplets measured at all three fractions (1,872 networks). In (a–c), some standard errors are smaller than the symbols. Small horizontal offsets in (a,b) separate the two cohorts. E quantities are blue, I quantities red. Measurements use 8,000 leak times, four distinct 500-time windows, and zero minimum gap.
