\section{A frozen-reservoir test of degree-induced variance heterogeneity}
This calculation isolates one possible source of the static variance seen
at nominally fixed degree. It is an exact sampling identity for a specified
reservoir matrix, followed by a conditional large-$N$ estimate. It is not a
complete stationary $N^{-1}$ correction for the recurrent network.

\subsection{One fixed-size subset}
Choose uniformly a subset of size $K$ from a pool of $M$ units. Write its
binary indicator as $A$, set $p=K/M$, $P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/M$, and
$z=A-p\mathbf1=PA$. For a real symmetric matrix $S$ define $B=PSP$,
$D=\operatorname{tr}B$, $T=\operatorname{tr}B^2$, and
$L=\sum_iB_{ii}^2$. For distinct indices the inclusion probabilities are
\[
 \pi_r=\frac{(K)_r}{(M)_r},\qquad r=1,2,3,4,\qquad \pi_1=p.
\]
The quadratic form is $z^TSz=A^TBA$. Since $B\mathbf1=0$,
$\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}=-D$, and hence
\[
 \mathbb E[A^TBA]=(p-\pi_2)D.
\]
To calculate the square, split the form into its diagonal part
$V=\sum_iB_{ii}A_i$ and off-diagonal part
$W=\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}A_iA_j$. The required contractions are
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E V^2&=pL+\pi_2(D^2-L),\\
 2\mathbb E VW&=-4\pi_2L-2\pi_3D^2+4\pi_3L,\\
 \mathbb E W^2&=\pi_2(2T-2L)+\pi_3(8L-4T)
                      +\pi_4(D^2+2T-6L).
\end{align*}
In the last equation, the three terms have two, three, and four distinct
indices, respectively. Adding them and subtracting the squared mean gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\operatorname{Var}(z^TSz)
 =2cT+\kappa L+[c-(p-\pi_2)^2]D^2,}
 \quad c=\pi_2-2\pi_3+\pi_4,\quad
 \kappa=p-7\pi_2+12\pi_3-6\pi_4.
 \label{eq:review_exact_fixed_qvar}
\end{equation}
For a constant diagonal of $B$, $L=D^2/M$, and the identity simplifies to
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}(z^TSz)=
 2c\left[T-\frac{D^2}{M-1}\right].
 \label{eq:review_const_diag_qvar}
\end{equation}
In particular, $S=sI$ has zero variance because $\|z\|^2=Mp(1-p)$ is fixed.
A Gaussian-row approximation that retained only $2\operatorname{tr}S^2$
would incorrectly assign a variance to this constant-diagonal contribution.

For $M\to\infty$ at fixed $p$, put $\sigma^2=p(1-p)$. Then
\[
 c=\sigma^4+O(M^{-1}),\quad
 \kappa=\sigma^2(1-6\sigma^2)+O(M^{-1}),\quad
 c-(p-\pi_2)^2=-\frac{\sigma^2(1-2p)^2}{M}+O(M^{-2}).
\]
The diagonal terms cancel to leading extensive order when $S_{ii}$ is
constant within the pool and its coherent projections are controlled.

\subsection{Independent presynaptic pools and the coherent row mean}
Let the full frozen coupling row be
$j=\bar j+\sum_b(t_b/\sqrt N)z_b$, with independent fixed-size subsets
in each pool. Define
\[
 V_b=\mathbb E[z_bz_b^T]
 =p_b(1-p_b)\frac{M_b}{M_b-1}P_b,\qquad
 B_b=P_bS_{bb}P_b,\quad \ell_b=(S\bar j)_b.
\]
The full quadratic form contains
\begin{align*}
 j^TSj={}&\bar j^TS\bar j
 +\sum_b\frac{2t_b}{\sqrt N}\ell_b^Tz_b
 +\sum_b\frac{t_b^2}{N}z_b^TS_{bb}z_b\\
 &+\sum_{b<c}\frac{2t_bt_c}{N}z_b^TS_{bc}z_c.
\end{align*}
Besides the diagonal-pool variance from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:review_exact_fixed_qvar}, the exact independent-pool terms are
\begin{align*}
 V_{\rm linear}&=\frac4N\sum_bt_b^2\ell_b^TV_b\ell_b,\\
 V_{\rm cross}&=\frac4{N^2}\sum_{b<c}t_b^2t_c^2
                  \operatorname{tr}(V_bS_{bc}V_cS_{cb}),\\
 2\operatorname{Cov}(V_{\rm lin},V_{\rm quad})
 &=\frac4{N^{3/2}}\sum_bt_b^3(p_b-3\pi_{2b}+2\pi_{3b})
                   \sum_i(P_b\ell_b)_i(B_b)_{ii}.
\end{align*}
All remaining cross-pool covariance terms vanish by independence and the
centering of every $z_b$. The last formula follows from
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\lambda^TA,A^TBA)
 =(p-3\pi_2+2\pi_3)\sum_i\lambda_iB_{ii},\qquad
 \mathbf1^T\lambda=0,
\]
using $B\mathbf1=0$. The cubic term vanishes for a constant projected
diagonal. The coherent linear variance is nonnegative and can remain
$O(N^{-1})$ when $\|S\bar j\|=O(1)$. Thus it is not automatically
subleading relative to the centered quadratic variance.

\subsection{The proposed leading frozen-row estimate}
Suppose $S$ has a population-constant leading diagonal $s_b$, bounded
operator norm, and controlled population projections. For a centered row,
the preceding formulas give
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}_{\rm frozen}(q_i)
 \simeq\frac2N\sum_{b,c}G_{ab}G_{ac}
 \left[\mathcal E^H_{bc}-\delta_{bc}f_b s_b^2\right],
 \quad
 \mathcal E^H_{bc}=\frac1N\|S_{bc}\|_F^2,
 \label{eq:review_frozen_Vq}
\end{equation}
where $G_{ab}=t_b^2p_b(1-p_b)$. For the input filter,
$S_{jk}=\int |H(\omega)|^2 C_{jk}(\omega)d\omega/(2\pi)$, and its block
energies are obtained by the corresponding two-frequency weights. This
checks the proposed diagonal subtraction and its factor of two.

The mean row must also be retained unless its filtered coherent contribution
is negligible at the claimed order. Uniformly small population projection
\emph{fractions} are enough for the leading pair energy, but do not by
themselves imply $S\bar j=o(1)$, which is what removes the linear term from
the leading frozen-row variance coefficient.

\subsection{Converting variance fluctuations into static heterogeneity}
At nominally fixed degree, linearizing the local mean rate around its
homogeneous operating point gives
\[
 \delta m_i=\alpha_a\delta\mu_i+\frac{\beta_a}{2}\delta q_i+\cdots,
 \qquad \beta_a=-\frac{h_a\alpha_a}{1+q_a}.
\]
If a further frozen-site decoupling makes
$\operatorname{Cov}(\delta\mu_i,\delta q_i)$ negligible at this order,
then a candidate static-variance estimate is
\[
 \Delta^{(q)}\simeq
 [I-W\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_a^2)]^{-1}
 W\operatorname{diag}(\beta_a^2/4)\,V_q.
\]
This final formula is conditional, rather than an exact consequence of the
subset calculation. A complete stationary coefficient would also require
reservoir reinsertion/self-response terms, dependence of $S$ on the probe
row, possible covariance between $\delta\mu$ and $\delta q$, source changes
and collective sample fluctuations, and consistent coherent-loop treatment.
They can enter at the same order as the local fluctuations being retained.
Stochastic count rounding also generates a separate static source. Agreement
or failure of this frozen diagnostic is informative; neither would complete
the finite-size correction to PR or explain the 60:40 discrepancy on its own.
