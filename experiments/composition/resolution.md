# Resolution of the 60:40 comparison within the paper's scope

The original apparent factor-two failure at 60:40 does not persist as a
systematic failure of the large-N prediction in the prospective size series.
The supported result is an empirical domain for the leading conditional EI
closure, together with a real finite-network limitation at $N=1000$. No fitted
covariance amplitude or completed analytic $N=1000$ correction is adopted.

## What was wrong with the original interpretation

The two original $N=1000$ networks are genuinely unusual; longer recordings do
not restore their PRs to the leading value. Earlier diagnostics distinguish a
slow collective mode in seed11 from the strongly reduced temporal activity
variance in seed22. Their similar PRs conceal different ingredients.

The expanded historical sixteen-network cohort has corrected mean PR/N
0.0256785 ± 0.0016272 network SE, against leading 0.0298935. Its remaining
14.1% difference cannot be dismissed as exactly zero. The old separately
centered-block estimator biased PR upward, so correcting that estimator does
not itself eliminate the physical discrepancy.

The new, independently specified four-network $N=1000$ pilot gives mean PR/N
0.0240983 at 60:40 and0.0303226 at 80:20, with broad network intervals. An 80:20
network also has PR/N 0.0156011. Thus the low-PR tail is not exclusive to the
60:40 composition. The small original samples were insufficient to identify
a composition-specific breakdown.

## What the prospective size series establishes

The primary protocol is fixed at $B=500$, $T=8000$, gap 0; all 40 planned composition
networks completed. Four independent seed clusters per composition were run
at $N=1000$, twelve at $N=2000$, and four at $N=4000$. Common-seed contrasts account for
pairing across sizes and fractions. The $N=2000$ extension remains separately
identifiable from its initial four-network pilot.

At $N=2000$ the observed mean relative errors in nu, covariance energy, and PR/N
are respectively [-0.60%,+1.65%,-1.59%] for 60:40 and
[-1.15%,+2.85%,-3.40%] for 80:20. All six pointwise conventional 95% network-mean
error intervals lie within ±15%. This is an explicitly quantified empirical
accuracy statement for the tested parameters and estimator. It is not a
simultaneous uniform 95% error bound or a convergence theorem.

The $N=4000$ comparison supports the dimensionality prediction but does not
establish monotone amplitude convergence. In the four 60:40 networks, nu is
5.11% above its leading value and energy is10.52% above, while PR differs by
only +0.265%. The95% relative energy-error interval is [-9.28%,+30.32%]. The
ratio is stable because its ingredients covary. The absolute ingredients
remain in the figures and cannot be declared controlled from this ratio.

Population geometry is independently informative. At $N=2000$, mean normalized
EE/EI/IE/II energy allocations differ from theory by 1.37% and 2.31% in relative
Frobenius norm; at $N=4000$ the errors are 0.64% and 0.91%. The separate within-E
and within-I PRs and the normalized cross-population energy also agree at the
reported network precision. Normalizing these energies is a secondary
geometry observable, not an adjustment of the PR amplitude.

## Where the finite-network local-law error enters

Static and temporal input variances fluctuate in opposite directions across
networks, although their sum is not fixed. The exact conditional Gaussian
functional has derivative dnu/dDelta=-A at fixed total input variance and
mean input. Its reference static partition direction has amplification 6.94.
The full DMFT degree tangent independently confirms Delta increasing while q
decreases. These statements explain why total marginal variance alone is an
insufficient validation of the temporal law; they do not predict the complete
quenched fluctuation distribution.

Measured time-averaged gain moments remain roughly 4–8% below the leading
values. Across 9 $N=2000$ networks per composition and all 4 $N=4000$ networks per
composition with compact local moments, a Gaussian marginal with each
neuron's measured mu_i and q_i predicts A to about 1% or better in group
means. Replacing those labels by a Gaussian static distribution and a common
population q produces appreciably larger errors. The intervening empirical
mu_i/common-q calculation separates that defined static-label and dynamic-
variance substitution path. Cross-block gain checks change A by less than 0.1%.
Thus the observed gain residual is principally a physical finite-network
local-law/distribution issue, rather than the gain-squaring estimator. These
are calibrated one-time marginal diagnostics, not a parameter-only repair or
validation of the full Gaussian trajectory response.

Exact static-source accounting further separates the empirical presynaptic
rate distribution, realized squared coupling weights, and centered cross-source
terms. Their sample means and network spread are reported separately. The
maximum mean-state equation RMS residual is $4.69\times10^{-4}$ in input
units; reconstructing the static variance from the measured rates and exact
connectivity differs from the measured static variance by at most
$2.46\times10^{-4}$.
No claim that every remaining collective contribution has been identified is made.

## What is retained as a limitation

The formal first stationary correction moves the $N=1000$ PR upward and does not
repair the observed lower cohort mean. Its individual ingredients also have
appreciable omitted terms. It remains an appendix calculation and coefficient
diagnostic, not the central validated prediction.

Measured-local-law substitutions previously repaired one outlier but failed
on other held-out networks. The population-uniform covariance sector is small
in the prospective cohort; this excludes that particular subspace as the whole
explanation, while nonuniform collective modes remain possible. There is no
completed parameter-only theory of every $N=1000$ realization or its rare tail.

This resolves the original comparison by delimiting the leading theory's
supported domain and explaining why the initial two-network result could not
establish an asymptotic 60:40 failure. A precise deterministic repair at $N=1000$
remains open and is not required by the agreed paper scope. Recording,
stationarity, and selected Lyapunov checks supplied by the runtime campaign
must be reported alongside these finite-record comparisons.
